# Broken Knight Royal Armor — Splash-Quality Target

These two images are the visual source of truth for the next Blender armor and hero-detail pass:

- `broken_knight_royal_armor_full_body_v1.png`
- `broken_knight_hero_face_v1.png`

The existing hero's body proportions and identity remain the base. The reference art defines the armor construction, material richness, head finish, and quality bar; it is not permission to replace the hero with a different body or face.

## Non-negotiable construction rules

1. Nothing may float. Every plate, trim piece, crest, ornament, and fastener must intersect or seat into a believable parent surface.
2. Armor coverage must flow continuously from helmet to gorget, cuirass, fauld/culet, tassets, cuisses, poleyns, greaves, and sabatons.
3. Articulation gaps are limited to armpits, inner elbows, groin, rear knees, and other real flexion zones; cover those gaps with fitted mail or leather.
4. Overlaps must follow movement direction:
   - gorget lames nest downward beneath the helmet and above the cuirass;
   - pauldron lames overlap down the upper arm without crossing the chest;
   - couters bridge rerebrace and vambrace;
   - fauld lames wrap around the waist into a rear culet;
   - tassets overlap cuisses;
   - poleyns overlap cuisses and greaves;
   - greaves overlap articulated sabatons.
5. Decorative crown geometry must be shallow embossed relief, engraved linework, rolled trim, or riveted construction—not separate hovering shapes.
6. Keep plate thickness visually credible and fitted to the hero. Avoid barrel torso volume, inflated limbs, thick legs, blob shoulders, or a feminine chest silhouette.
7. No exposed shoulder, neck, upper-back, butt, or upper-thigh holes when viewed from front, side, or rear.

## Form target

- Anatomical breastplate with raised center ridge, shaped masculine pectoral planes, tapered waist, and curved lower edge.
- Multi-lame pauldrons that cup the deltoids and remain clear of the chest.
- Close nested gorget that removes the visible head/neck seam.
- Fauld and rear culet built as a continuous wrap rather than separate front and back flaps.
- Fitted arm and leg defenses with readable elbow, wrist, knee, and ankle articulation.
- Helmet built as one connected shell with an integrated crown brow and functional face opening.
- Crimson plume made from grouped fibers with irregular silhouette and volume, not a solid slab.

## Material target

- Base metal: very dark blued/navy forged steel with subtle cobalt character.
- Trim/inlay: aged royal gold or brass, warm and worn rather than bright yellow.
- Flexible zones: blackened mail and dark brown leather.
- Surface response: controlled roughness, fine hammer texture, micro-scratches, restrained edge wear, readable rivets and hinges.
- Avoid white armor, bright cyan, mirror shine, plastic gloss, and broad featureless color fields.

## Face and hair target

- Preserve the current hero identity while improving anatomy and realism.
- Warm neutral human complexion with restrained pores, slight color variation, faint beard shadow, and natural lip color.
- Eyes sit inside real lids and sockets; eyebrows emerge from the skin; mouth, nostrils, ears, jaw, and chin remain fully modeled.
- Head, neck, trapezius, clavicles, and upper torso must transition continuously with no seam or shelf.
- Short dark hair uses grouped strands, varied flow, broken silhouette, natural density, and a tapered side/back profile—no cap, plugs, bald spot, or repeated spikes.

## Acceptance views

Before promotion, render front, rear, side, and three-quarter views in neutral lighting plus one close-up each of the helmet/gorget, shoulder/armpit, waist/rear culet, elbow/wrist, knee/ankle, and face/hair. Reject the pass if any plate floats, exposes an unintended gap, clips through the hero, or breaks its overlap order.
